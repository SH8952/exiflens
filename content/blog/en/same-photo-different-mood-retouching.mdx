In this post I put the same photo side by side, retouched in two different ways. The shooting settings are identical, but the mood is completely different. ## Intent Starts the Moment You Press the Shutter

What are you thinking about when you press the camera shutter?

Intent starts not at the retouching stage but before you press the shutter. What composition you will use, whether to preserve the bright sky or keep the dark areas, and at which moment to press are all intent. Retouching is only the last stage laid on top; it does not create intent from scratch.

## Making the Intent Known Is the Photographer's Job

Only the photographer who presses the shutter can know the intent of a photo, but making that intent known to many people is entirely the photographer's role and something they must work at.

Viewers cannot know what the photographer felt; they only see the result. So you have to convey the intent through visual cues such as color, brightness, and contrast. Retouching is not turning a photo into something else; it is closer to refining the photo so that what the photographer felt reaches the viewer.

## How Retouching Changes the Mood

The same result can have a different mood depending on how it is retouched. The factors that decide the mood can be broadly divided into a few.

| Retouch element | If you do this | You get this mood |
|---|---|---|
| Color temperature (white balance) | Make it bluer | Cold, quiet feeling |
| Color temperature (white balance) | Make it yellower | Warm, cozy feeling |
| Contrast (tone curve) | Raise it | Dramatic, intense feeling |
| Contrast (tone curve) | Lower it | Soft, calm feeling |
| Saturation | Raise it | Vivid, lively feeling |
| Saturation | Lower it | Vintage, lonely feeling |
| Shadows and highlights | Brighten the shadows | Details show, light feeling |
| Shadows and highlights | Leave the shadows dark | Depth and weight |
| Color grading | Tint highlights and shadows with different colors | Cinematic color |
| Vignetting | Darken the edges | The eye gathers toward the center |

## Things Worth Deciding Before You Retouch

1. **Write your intent in one sentence.** Short is fine, like "a lonely evening sea" or "contrast between light and shadow." With a direction, your retouching won't wobble.
2. **Change one thing at a time.** If you adjust in the order color temperature, contrast, saturation, you can tell what changed the mood.
3. **Keep the original (RAW).** Retouching should always be reversible.
4. **Check with the histogram.** You can see whether highlights are blown to white or shadows are crushed.
5. **Take time and look again.** Looking the next day, you'll see the parts that were overdone.
6. **Make the same photo two ways and compare.** Placing them side by side as in this post makes the mood you want clearer.

## FAQ

**Doesn't retouching make a photo fake?**

Digital cameras already process color and contrast when they make a JPEG at the shooting stage. Retouching is the photographer making that choice directly. However, for photos where facts matter, such as documentation or news, it is better to avoid heavy retouching.

**Which retouch is the right answer?**

There is none. The standard is whether the intended mood reaches the viewer.

**If I'm just starting to retouch, where should I begin?**

Start by adjusting exposure, white balance, and contrast. Those three alone change the mood a lot.

**Are retouching values saved in EXIF too?**

No. EXIF records shooting information such as camera, lens, shutter speed, aperture, and ISO. Retouching values are managed separately in the editing program.

## Putting It Together

- The same scene gets a different mood depending on color temperature, contrast, saturation, and the brightness of shadows.
- What matters is not technique but "what do you want to convey?"
- Decide your intent first, before you press the shutter and before you retouch. Then the same photo can be finished in the mood you want.
